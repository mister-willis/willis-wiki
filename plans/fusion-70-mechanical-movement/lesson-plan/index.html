    Lesson Plan — Mechanical Movement Project

    Planbook Fields

    Lesson (Day 1):

    Direct instruction with slides introducing mechanisms as motion converters (input, output), the rules a chosen movement must pass, the 112 mm display base, and the fits-to-joints table. Students browse 507 Mechanical Movements, get a movement approved, and start a notebook entry with the movement record, parts list, joint table, and an empty exceptions table, then create their Fusion project folder. Fusion modeling does not begin. Deliverable in progress; scored 0–4 with the whole project on the rubric in the assignment.

    Lesson (Day 2):

    Work day. Students model the 112 × 112 × 4 mm base with the holes and slots from their joint table, then begin the remaining parts, one Part Design per printed piece, each oriented as it will print. Teacher circulates with notebook check-ins: joint table against the holes on screen. Scored at the end of the project.

    Lesson (Day 3):

    Work day. Students continue modeling parts from their parts list, sizing every hole and pin from the joint table's gap per side and checking each finished part against the eight design rules. Exceptions are recorded as they arise. Scored at the end of the project.

    Lesson (Day 4):

    Work day. Students finish modeling every part on the parts list; the optional gear section (Spur Gear plugin, Motion Link) applies to students whose movement has gears. Target by the bell: every row of the parts list has a saved Part Design. Scored at the end of the project.

    Lesson (Day 5):

    Work day. Students create the assembly beside the Parts folder, ground only the base, and work down the joint table creating and naming joints, then drive the input through a full cycle. Target: the mechanism runs from input to output. Scored at the end of the project.

    Lesson (Day 6):

    Work day. Students fix what the assembly exposed, run Interference on the assembly and Draft Analysis on each part, and complete the exceptions table with a reason for every rule their design breaks. Scored at the end of the project.

    Lesson (Day 7):

    Work day. Students create a dimensioned drawing of every unique part on the NPA Drawing Template, export each as a PDF into the notebook entry, and add a screenshot of the running assembly with named joints. Self-check against the Short List in the assignment. Scored at the end of the project.

    Lesson (Day 8):

    Student demonstrations, one minute each in drawn order, showing the Fusion assembly running and describing the movement's input, output, and conversion. Deliverables: Fusion project folder, notebook entry, and the demonstration. Scored 0–4 on the rubric in the assignment.

    Standards: AZ CTE-ENG 2.4, 2.5, 2.8, 3.3, 5.4, 5.7, 7.5, 7.2 (partial)

    Learning Objectives

    Students will be able to:

    • State a mechanism's input motion and output motion and explain why a device whose output matches its input is not a mechanism.
    • Decompose a movement from an engraving and animation into a parts list and a joint table, choosing a Fusion joint type and a fit (press, close, free) for every pair of touching parts.
    • Model each printed piece as its own Part Design, sizing holes and pins from the gap-per-side rule and orienting each part as it will print.
    • Assemble the parts with named joints so that driving the input produces the movement's output, and verify the design with Interference and Draft Analysis.
    • Record every deliberate departure from the design rules with a reason.
    • Produce a dimensioned drawing of every unique part on the NPA Drawing Template.
    • Demonstrate the assembly and describe the movement in one minute.

    Standards Alignment

    Code Standard (abbreviated) Why it's defensible here
    CTE-ENG 5.4 Use software tools to model, analyze, and/or design solutions (Fusion named in the standard). Every part, the assembly, the Interference and Draft Analysis checks, and the drawings are Fusion work with no recipe.
    CTE-ENG 2.4 Select the best solution within the constraints and criteria. Students choose a movement against five stated rules, the base spec, the print limits, and the no-fasteners rule; approval (step 2) checks the choice against the criteria before any modeling.
    CTE-ENG 2.5 Develop a prototype or model to test the selected solution. The Fusion assembly is the model, and driving the input through a cycle is the test; Interference and Draft Analysis are the second test. Part II prints it.
    CTE-ENG 2.8 Document and report all results. The notebook entry (movement record, parts list, joint table, exceptions table, screenshot, drawings) is the documentation; the Day 8 demonstration is the report.
    CTE-ENG 3.3 Display data graphically using diagrams and working drawings. Every unique part gets a dimensioned drawing on the class template, required for the whole class, not a challenge.
    CTE-ENG 5.7 Review fabrication methods to create potential solutions (3D printing named). The design is checked against the 3D-printing design rules part by part, and any rule broken must be justified in the exceptions table.
    CTE-ENG 7.5 Document and present project results/outcomes. The notebook entry documents; the demonstration presents the running assembly and its input-to-output story.
    CTE-ENG 7.2 Utilize time-management techniques (planning, goals, scheduling). Partial: the eight-day pacing table is a schedule students check themselves against, and Day 3's do-now asks for a count of remaining parts. Nothing is turned in that shows the scheduling itself.

    Not claimed: CTE-ENG 5.1 (tolerance). The fits table is a tolerance scheme, but nothing is measured until Part II; claim it there.

    Materials & Setup

    • slides.pptx (this folder), Day 1 only.
    • Student computers with Autodesk Fusion and student accounts signed in. The Spur Gear plugin is installed per the wiki page by any student whose movement needs it; expect two or three per class.
    • Notebooks (the year-long Google Doc), open before Fusion on Day 1.
    • The NPA Drawing Template in the root of Class Files; confirm it's there before Day 7.
    • Wiki pages open on the projector on Day 1: 507movements.com, the Design Rules for 3D Printing page, and the Fusion Spur Gear Plug-in page.
    • Students should have the slider-crank assembly (lab 50) and the hand crank inspection (lab 60) still in their student folders; the rules slide and the your-turn slide refer back to both.
    • A hat, or anything that draws names, for Day 8.
    • Office supplies on request (rubber bands, string) for students whose movement needs a return spring; not needed until Part II, but know the answer when they ask on Day 1.

    Slides & Direct Instruction

    The deck (9 slides, speaker notes on each, Day 1 only) runs: title with the four numbers (1868, 507, 112 mm, 8 days) → what counts as a mechanism, with an input/output table that ends on a wheel that isn't one → the five rules as tiles, plus "pick an animated one" → the base, drawn, with its spec table → the fits-to-joints table → "your turn" on the slider-crank and a wheel with a handle → the eight-day pacing table → the mission slide (Turn It In verbatim) → the Short List. The deck is short by design; seven of the eight days are work days and the lab carries the checkpoints. Questions worth asking:

    • "What was the input on the slider-crank, and what was the output?" (The crank; the slider. Around becomes back-and-forth, which is the whole definition.)
    • "A wheel with a handle: mechanism or not?" (Not. Output equals input. Add a second wheel of a different size touching it and now it is.)
    • "Why does an axle get modeled as its own part instead of growing out of the base?" (It would print standing up, layer lines across the load, and snap. Lying down it's strong, and a 0.1 mm press fit holds it.)
    • "Why 0.3 mm per side and not 0.3 mm total?" (Per side means the hole is 0.6 mm bigger across. Per side is the trap; say it twice.)

    After the slides, put 507movements.com on the projector, click one color thumbnail, and count out loud with the class: input, output, moving parts, joint types. Then click a trivial one and let them tell you why it fails. Release them to steps 1 through 7. Fusion stays closed until step 7 (the project folder), and the notebook tables come first; say so.

    Pacing — Eight 51-Minute Periods

    Day 1 — Choose and plan

    Minutes Segment What happens
    0–4 Settle + do-now On the board: "Name the three moving parts of the slider-crank you built in the joints lab." One answer aloud.
    4–20 Slides Direct instruction. Land the input/output test and "per side" hard; the rubric's 2 row and every binding mechanism come from those two.
    20–24 Live count One animated movement on the projector, counted out loud against the rules; one trivial one rejected. "Understand your mission for today?"
    24–46 Lab (steps 1–7) Students browse, choose, and bring you the page for approval. Check input, output, moving parts, joint types, part count. Then the notebook tables and the project folder.
    46–51 Wrap Cold-close: "What's the first part you'll model tomorrow after the base, and what fit does it have?" Preview: "Tomorrow the base gets its holes, and every hole comes from your joint table."

    Day 2 — The base and first parts

    Minutes Segment What happens
    0–4 Settle + do-now "Open your joint table. How many holes does your base need, and what gap does each one get?"
    4–8 Offset demo Two-minute demo: project the mating part's edge into a sketch and Offset by the gap. This is the one modeling move that's new.
    8–45 Lab (steps 8–9) Base modeled and saved in Parts, then the next parts. Circulate: joint table versus the holes on screen; every hole diameter should be a table row plus two gaps.
    45–51 Wrap Save. Cold-close: "Which part are you least sure will print, and which rule is it?" Preview: "Tomorrow, more parts and the first exceptions."

    Day 3 — Parts

    Minutes Segment What happens
    0–4 Settle + do-now "Count the rows of your parts list without a saved Part Design. That's your number for today."
    4–45 Lab (steps 9–12) Modeling. Each finished part gets a rules check (step 10); exceptions go in the table with a reason as they're found, not on Day 6. Students behind pace get a triage conversation: which parts does the movement need, which are detail.
    45–51 Wrap Save. Cold-close: "Name one part you oriented differently on the bed than you first planned, and why." Preview: "Tomorrow is the last modeling day. Gears, if you have them."

    Day 4 — Finish the parts

    Minutes Segment What happens
    0–4 Settle + do-now "Which parts still aren't saved? If the answer is none, open the gear section or start reading the assembly steps."
    4–45 Lab (finish; gear section) Remaining parts. Students with gears install the plugin and generate gears with the bore sized from the joint table. Finished students may start the assembly early.
    45–51 Wrap Save. Cold-close: "How many rows are in your joint table? That's how many joints you're building tomorrow." Preview: "Tomorrow it moves, or it tells you which hole is wrong."

    Day 5 — Assemble

    Minutes Segment What happens
    0–4 Settle + do-now "Say the name of your input joint and your output part to a neighbor."
    4–8 Order demo Two minutes: assembly saved beside Parts, base grounded, then joints in the order the motion flows, input to output.
    8–42 Lab (steps 13–17) Joints from the table, one row at a time, named as they're made. Circulate: a joint that won't line up is a part problem; send them to edit the part, not to fight the joint.
    42–48 Drive it (step 17) Everyone drives the input through a cycle. Parts passing through each other get noted for tomorrow.
    48–51 Wrap Save. Cold-close: "What did the assembly find that the parts didn't?" Preview: "Tomorrow is fixing, Interference, and Draft Analysis."

    Day 6 — Prove it will print

    Minutes Segment What happens
    0–4 Settle + do-now "Drag your input once. Does the output do what your movement record says?" Yes moves on; no is today's first job.
    4–30 Lab (fixes; step 18) Fixes from Day 5, then Interference on the assembly. Any result is a fit sized wrong; fix the part.
    30–45 Lab (steps 19–20) Draft Analysis on each part, blue faces decided, exceptions table finished with reasons a printer would accept.
    45–51 Wrap Save. Cold-close: "Read me one exception and its reason." (Or "none," which is also an answer.) Preview: "Tomorrow, drawings. NPA template. The wrong one gets sent back."

    Day 7 — Drawings

    Minutes Segment What happens
    0–4 Settle + do-now "How many unique parts do you have? That's how many drawings you owe today."
    4–8 Template demo Create a drawing From Design, choose the template option, browse to the NPA Drawing Template in the root of Class Files, then OK. Show the default ASME sheet once so they recognize the mistake.
    8–42 Lab (steps 21–23) Drawings, PDFs into the notebook entry, assembly screenshot with joint names. Circulate: every hole and pin diameter dimensioned, every hole located.
    42–48 Short List Everyone reads the Short List at the end of the assignment against their own work. Anything false is tomorrow morning's job before the bell.
    48–51 Wrap Draw and post the demonstration order so nobody is surprised at the bell. Preview: "One minute each. Fusion on the screen. Describe the movement the way the site does."

    Day 8 — Demonstrations

    Minutes Segment What happens
    0–3 Settle First presenter loads the assembly while the class settles. Class rule on the board: one question per presenter, asked by someone who hasn't asked yet.
    3–46 Demonstrations One minute plus a question, about a minute and a half per student. Run a visible timer. Score on the rubric as they go; input, output, and conversion named is the row to watch.
    46–51 Wrap Cold-close: "Which movement in the room would you print first, and why?" Preview the printing lab: "Next, you learn the printers. Then these come off the screen."

    A class larger than about twenty-five spills demonstrations into a ninth day; plan the overflow as a work day for students who need it and demonstrations for the rest.

    ELEOT: The 20-Minute Window

    Item The move in this lesson
    1 The Day 1 live count has the class calling out input, output, and moving parts for a movement on the projector; approval at the desk requires each student to point at the same things on their own pick.
    2 The your-turn slide is decided with a neighbor before answers are taken; Day 5's do-now has students naming their input joint to a neighbor.
    5 The notebook tables (movement record, parts list, joint table, exceptions) are the organizers the whole build runs on; the Design Rules page and the fits table are open on every screen.
    7 Fusion is used to design original parts, assemble and drive them, and test the design with Interference and Draft Analysis, not to copy a class file.
    9 Decomposing an engraving into parts and joints, choosing a fit for each pair, and justifying each exception are analysis and evaluation; approval at the desk is where the analysis gets checked.
    10 Every movement is a real mechanism from a real engineering catalog, and the design is headed for a real printer and a real wall in the next project.
    11 Six work days against a posted pacing table with a day-by-day target; Day 6 exists because the assembly exposes problems that have to be fixed rather than abandoned.
    12 Students choose the movement, write the plan, and manage the modeling days; the triage conversation on Day 3 is theirs to decide.
    15 Off-menu is the only menu: every choice is the student's own, and the exceptions table makes "I broke a rule on purpose, here's why" a normal, written thing to say.
    18 Every day opens with a one-line do-now that names the day's number (parts left, joints to build, drawings owed); the pacing table is posted.
    22 Rubric in the assignment, referenced on Day 1 for approval and on Day 7 with the Short List; Interference and Draft Analysis results are feedback the student acts on before Day 7.
    23 The parts list and joint table are checklists the student works down; the Short List on Day 7 is a self-assessment against every deliverable.
    24 The exceptions table asks for the reasoning behind each departure; the demonstration asks the student to explain how input becomes output.

    Common Student Errors

    These are predicted from the design and the lab's warning callouts, not observed in class; revise after the first teach.

    • A wheel with a handle, or two wheels on one axle. Presents at approval as a movement whose output matches its input. Ask "what does the output do that the input doesn't?" and send them back to the site.
    • Uncounted frame parts. The engraving's bracket or fixed pin is missed, so the parts list is short and the assembly has nothing to attach a lever to. Catch at approval: "point at every part in the drawing."
    • Gap applied once instead of per side. A 6 mm axle in a 6.3 mm hole. Presents as Interference results on Day 6, or a joint table that says 0.3 mm and a hole that measures 6.30. Measure the hole with them.
    • Axle grown out of the base. A post extruded from the base plate, which will print standing up and snap. Presents on Day 2. Point at the Strength rule and the base slide's "plate with holes."
    • Assembly saved inside Parts, or a second grounded part. The mechanism won't move, or nothing moves. Check the Browser for one anchor icon and the Data Panel for the assembly beside Parts.
    • Joint on the wrong axis. A revolute joint whose axis is across the hole instead of through it; the part swings like a door. Right-click the joint, edit, and re-pick the circular edge.
    • Draft Analysis still showing when Interference or a section is run. Fusion won't show a section cut while an analysis is visible. Hide Draft1 in the Browser's Analysis folder.
    • The default ASME drawing sheet. OK clicked before choosing the template option. Presents on Day 7 as a sheet with no NPA title block. Delete the drawing and start over; there's no way to swap the template after the fact.
    • Exceptions table left blank because "nothing broke." A design with a 2.5 mm pin and no row for it. Ask them to read their pin diameters aloud against the 3 mm minimum.

    Assessment & Evidence

    • Deliverable: Fusion project folder Movement <number> - <short name> with a Parts folder (one Part Design per printed piece) and the Movement <number> assembly beside it; notebook entry Mechanical Movement Project with the movement record, parts list, joint table, exceptions table (or "None"), a screenshot of the running assembly with named joints, and a PDF drawing of every unique part on the NPA Drawing Template; the one-minute demonstration (see Turn It In).
    • Score: 0–4 holistic rubric in the assignment. A 4 is a movement that passes every rule, one Part Design per piece with fits from a complete joint table, an assembly that runs input to output with every joint named and Interference empty, every broken rule in the exceptions table with a reason, a dimensioned drawing of every unique part on the NPA template, and a demonstration that names the input, the output, and the conversion.
    • Look-fors during check-ins: a joint table with the fit and gap columns filled before any part has a hole; hole diameters that equal a table row plus two gaps; parts modeled with the chosen face on XY; a Data Panel with the assembly beside Parts and one anchor icon in the Browser; joint names that match the table; an exceptions table with reasons a printer would accept; drawings with a title block; a student who can name the input and output without looking at the screen.
