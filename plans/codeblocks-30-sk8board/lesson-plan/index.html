    Lesson Plan — sk8board Time!

    Planbook Fields

    Lesson (Day 1):

    Direct instruction with slides: skateboard anatomy (deck, kick, truck, axle, wheelbase), the six variables that will drive the design, and the Copy block — build a sub-assembly once, copy and rotate it into place. Students start a new Codeblocks project, create the six dimension variables, and build both axle-and-truck assemblies by reading the block screenshots without step-by-step hand-holding.

    Lesson (Day 2):

    Brief recap: what the Copy block saved us on Day 1. Students build the deck with a kick at each end (copying and rotating the nose kick to make the tail), add four snap-on wheels that print as separate pieces, and export the board as an .STL submitted in Google Classroom. Scored 0–4 on the rubric printed in the assignment.

    Standards: AZ CS 6.AP.V.1, 6.AP.M.1, 6.AP.M.2

    Learning Objectives

    Students will be able to:

    • Read a block screenshot and reproduce the program it shows.
    • Define variables named for real dimensions and use them across a design.
    • Use the Copy block to reuse a built sub-assembly instead of rebuilding it.
    • Decompose a multi-part model into assemblies built and tested separately.

    Standards Alignment

    Code Standard (abbreviated) Why it's defensible here
    6.AP.V.1 Identify variables and perform operations on their values. Six variables named for real skateboard dimensions (width, deck length, kick length, wheelbase, wheel diameters) drive the whole build; naming things for what they mean is the standard's readability point.
    6.AP.M.1 Decompose problems into parts to facilitate design, implementation, and review. The board is built as separate assemblies — axles and trucks, deck, wheels — each finished and checked against its preview screenshot before the next starts.
    6.AP.M.2 Use procedures to organize code and make it easier to reuse. Partial, and a cross-tool analogy: the Copy block reuses a built group (wheel-end, front axle, nose kick) the way a procedure reuses instructions — write once, reuse three times — but there are no true parameterized procedures in Codeblocks. Prune if you want M.2 reserved for real functions.

    Materials & Setup

    • slides.pptx (this folder) loaded and tested.
    • Tinkercad accounts working (verified back in lab 10); this is a new project, not a continuation.
    • Google Classroom assignment open for the .STL.
    • Notebooks.
    • Optional but worth it: a real fingerboard or skateboard photo on the side screen for the anatomy talk.

    Slides & Direct Instruction

    The deck (8 slides, speaker notes on each) runs: title → the training wheels come off (reading screenshots is the skill) → skateboard anatomy vocabulary → a dark "build one, copy the rest" beat → the six variables and what each controls → predict-the-change practice pair → Day 2 build plan (deck, kicks, wheels, export) → mission slide. Day 1 stops after the practice slide; the build-plan slide opens Day 2. Questions worth asking:

    • "The lab shows you a picture of blocks instead of telling you each step. What do you have to do differently?" (read every parameter, not skim — the picture is the instruction)
    • "Why build the axle once and copy it, instead of building it twice?" (half the work, and a fix to one is a fix to both — until you copy it again, so fix first, copy second)
    • "Why do the wheels print as separate pieces?" (a printed-together wheel is a fused wheel; separate pieces snap on and actually spin)

    Pacing — Two 51-Minute Periods

    Day 1

    Minutes Segment What happens
    0–4 Settle + do-now On the board: "Label a skateboard: deck, truck, axle, wheelbase. Guess if you don't know." Cheap vocabulary pre-check.
    4–18 Slides Slides 1–6; spend the time on the anatomy and Copy-block slides — those two carry the day.
    18–46 Lab Steps 1–9: variables, then both axle-and-truck assemblies. Reading the torus and hole-box screenshots carefully is the work; resist reading them aloud for students.
    46–51 Wrap Compare screens to the axles preview. Cold-close: "How many times did you build a wheel-end? How many exist?" (once; four). Preview: tomorrow the deck and wheels.

    Day 2

    Minutes Segment What happens
    0–4 Settle + do-now "The kick at the back of your board — will you build it or copy it? From what?" (copy the nose kick, rotate it around)
    4–10 Slides Slide 7: deck, kicks, wheels, export — the day in one picture.
    10–40 Lab Steps 10–13: deck, both kicks, four wheels. Kick rotation direction is the sticking point — the preview screenshot settles arguments.
    40–46 Export + submit Steps 14–16: Share → .STL → Google Classroom.
    46–51 Wrap Cold-close: "You built maybe half the shapes on your screen. Where did the rest come from?" Preview: "Next lab there are no screenshots at all. Your design, your rules, one battle."

    ELEOT: The 20-Minute Window

    Item The move in this lesson
    B.1 The overview names the bar out loud — "the training wheels are off" — and students can restate what that demands of them.
    B.2 Screenshot-reading is a deliberate step up from numbered steps, with preview images every few steps so the challenge stays attainable.
    B.4 Reproducing a program from its picture is analysis — students must decode what each parameter does rather than follow a sentence.
    B.5 Two days of self-directed building from the doc; the teacher demos nothing after the slides.
    C.3 Preview screenshots at each assembly boundary are the built-in rescue; neighbors second, teacher third.
    D.2 Every variable is a real dimension of a real object they've ridden or seen — wheelbase isn't an abstraction when it's on the do-now.
    E.1 Rotating notebook check-ins both days, per the standing system.
    E.3 Both wraps have learners verbalizing the reuse idea (built once, copied) — the lesson's concept in their own words.
    E.4 Rubric printed in the assignment; the 4-row's "changing width rebuilds the board" gives students a self-test they can run before submitting.
    G.2 An original multi-part printable artifact designed entirely in code.

    Common Student Errors

    • Skimming a screenshot and missing one parameter (a radius, a rotation axis) — the model looks almost right, which is worse than wrong. Teach "diff your blocks against the picture" as the first debugging move.
    • Copying before the sub-assembly is finished — the copy doesn't inherit later fixes, and the two halves drift apart.
    • Wheel inner diameter changed without matching the axle — the wheel won't snap on. The 5.5 mm isn't decoration; it's a fit.
    • Kicks rotated the wrong direction, curving down like a scoop instead of up.
    • Forgetting Group Selection before moving an assembly, so the move grabs half the parts and leaves the rest behind.

    Assessment & Evidence

    • Deliverable: the fingerboard's .STL file submitted in Google Classroom (mirrors Turn It In).
    • Score: 0–4 holistic rubric printed in the assignment. A 4 requires both axle assemblies, kicks both ends, four separate wheels, and variables that genuinely drive the dimensions.
    • Look-fors during check-ins: the six Set blocks at the top; Copy blocks where the lab expects reuse (wheel-end, rear axle, tail kick); student can point to which parts move when wheelbase changes.
