    Lesson Plan — Expired Patent Project

    Planbook Fields

    Lesson (Day 1):

    Direct instruction with slides introducing patents as published specifications: drawings, description, and claims, and the two ways a patent expires (full term or unpaid maintenance fees). Students select an expired patent from the menu in the assignment (or propose one for approval), record its filing, grant, and expiration dates in their project notebooks, extract the elements of claim 1 into a checklist, and write a modeling plan before opening Fusion. Deliverable: notebook entry with dates, element list, and plan. Scored with the whole project, 0–4, on the rubric in the assignment.

    Lesson (Day 2):

    Work day. Students create their Fusion part design, place the patent drawing as a calibrated canvas, and model the invention's main body from dimensioned sketches. Teacher circulates with notebook check-ins against each student's element list. Deliverable in progress; scored at the end of the project.

    Lesson (Day 3):

    Work day. Students complete the remaining claim 1 elements, rename sketches, bodies, and features so the timeline reads as a build story, and play the timeline back to catch out-of-order or failing features. Target by the bell: every claim 1 element present. Scored at the end of the project.

    Lesson (Day 4):

    Work day and presentation prep. Students finish the model, optionally add an appearance and render or a Fusion drawing sheet, and prepare a three-minute presentation (invention, history with dates and how the patent expired, live timeline playback, tools used). Practice run of the timeline reveal required before leaving. Scored at the end of the project.

    Lesson (Day 5):

    Student presentations, three minutes each in drawn order, with one question from the class. Deliverables: Fusion design saved to the Fusion Labs project, notebook entry, and the presentation. Scored 0–4 on the rubric in the assignment.

    Standards: AZ CTE-ENG 2.5, 5.4, 6.2, 7.5, 3.3 (partial, Drawing challenge only)

    Learning Objectives

    Students will be able to:

    • Locate a patent's filing, grant, and expiration dates and state whether it expired at full term or lapsed for unpaid maintenance fees.
    • Read claim 1 of a patent and list the elements a faithful model must contain.
    • Write a modeling plan (target size, first element, expected tool per element) before modeling.
    • Build a multi-feature Fusion model from dimensioned sketches over a calibrated canvas, with named sketches, bodies, and features.
    • Present the patent's history and the build in three minutes, narrating a live timeline playback by design decision rather than by click.

    Standards Alignment

    Code Standard (abbreviated) Why it's defensible here
    CTE-ENG 5.4 Use software tools to model, analyze, and/or design solutions (Fusion 360 named in the standard). The whole project is a Fusion model built from dimensioned sketches, patterns, shell, and revolve, without a step-by-step recipe.
    CTE-ENG 2.5 Develop a prototype or model to test the selected solution. Students select an invention from a menu of solutions, then develop a CAD model of it; the model is checked against the patent's claim 1 and drawings, which is the test. Not the students' own solution, and the plan says so.
    CTE-ENG 6.2 Utilize the three stages of oral presentation (planning, practicing, presenting). Day 4 requires a written outline against the four-part table and a practiced timeline run; Day 5 is the presentation. All three stages are scheduled and observable.
    CTE-ENG 7.5 Document and present project results/outcomes. The notebook entry (dates, element list, plan, screenshot) is the documentation; the Day 5 presentation is the reporting.
    CTE-ENG 3.3 Display data graphically using diagrams and working drawings. Partial: only students who complete the Fusion Drawing challenge (multi-view sheet with dimensions) produce a working drawing. Claim it for those students; not for the class.

    Materials & Setup

    • slides.pptx (this folder), used on Day 1 only.
    • Student computers with Autodesk Fusion installed and student accounts signed in; confirm the Fusion Labs project is shared with the class hub.
    • Notebooks (the year-long Google Doc), needed on Day 1 before any Fusion work.
    • Open the Google Patents pages for the six menu patents yourself before Day 1 and confirm they load on the school network; the whole selection step depends on it.
    • A hat, or anything that draws names, for presentation order on Day 5.
    • Optional: a printed copy of one claim 1 (the banana case is the clearest) for the Day 1 claim-reading demo.

    Slides & Direct Instruction

    The deck (6 slides, speaker notes on each, Day 1 only) runs: chicken glasses hook → what a patent trades (publication for a monopoly, then the public domain) → the three parts of a patent, with claim 1 as the parts list → the two ways a patent dies, using the menu's dates → "your turn" reading one claim 1 aloud and pulling out the nouns → mission slide. The deck is short by design; four of the five days are work days and the lab document carries the checkpoints. Questions worth asking:

    • "Why would an inventor publish exactly how the thing works?" (The deal: publication in exchange for the monopoly. Without the publication there's no patent, only a secret.)
    • "Three of the six menu patents were abandoned before their term ended. Why would anyone stop paying to keep a patent alive?" (Nobody was buying the greenhouse helmet. Maintenance fees make the owner decide whether the patent is worth more than the fee.)
    • "If claim 1 of the banana case names a hinge and your model has no hinge, what did you build?" (A banana-shaped box. The claim is the specification, and the model is checked against it, which is how every engineering deliverable gets checked.)

    After the slides, project the banana case's claim 1 and pull the nouns out of it live, in about two minutes, writing the element list on the board the way students will build theirs in their notebooks (the lab gives them a starter table to copy). Then release them to steps 1 through 5 of the assignment. Fusion stays closed on Day 1 until the plan is written; say so.

    Pacing — Five 51-Minute Periods

    Day 1 — Select and plan

    Minutes Segment What happens
    0–4 Settle + do-now On the board: "Name one tool from the Fusion tutorials you'd use to make a hollow dome, and one you'd use to make a fork's tines." One answer each aloud.
    4–18 Slides Direct instruction; land the claim-1-as-parts-list idea hard, since the rubric's 2 row is built on it.
    18–21 Claim demo Project the banana case claim 1, extract the nouns live onto the board. "Understand your mission for today?"
    21–45 Lab (steps 1–5) Students choose, record dates, list elements, write the plan. Approve off-menu patents at your desk, checking legal status and claim length.
    45–51 Wrap Cold-close: "What's the first element you'll model tomorrow, and why that one first?" Preview: "Tomorrow the drawing goes into Fusion as a canvas, and you sketch on top of it."

    Day 2 — Main body

    Minutes Segment What happens
    0–4 Settle + do-now "Find your element list in your notebook. Which element is the one everything else attaches to?" That's today's target.
    4–8 Canvas demo Two-minute demo of Insert ▸ Canvas and Calibrate; this is the one new tool in the project.
    8–45 Lab (steps 6–8) File created and named, canvas placed, main body modeled. Circulate with notebook check-ins: plan versus what's on screen.
    45–51 Wrap Save. Cold-close: "Which dimension did you have to invent because the drawing didn't give it to you?" Preview: "Tomorrow, the rest of the list, and your timeline gets names."

    Day 3 — Every element

    Minutes Segment What happens
    0–4 Settle + do-now "Count the unchecked elements on your list. That's your number for today."
    4–40 Lab (steps 9–11) Remaining elements, renaming as they go. Students behind the pacing table get a triage conversation: which elements are essential to claim 1, which are detail.
    40–48 Timeline playback (step 12) Everyone drags the marker left and plays it forward. Red flags get fixed by editing the earlier feature, not patching at the end.
    48–51 Wrap Save. Cold-close: "Name the feature you'd move earlier in your timeline if you started over." Preview: "Tomorrow is finishing and presentation prep; the challenges open up if you're done."

    Day 4 — Finish and prepare

    Minutes Segment What happens
    0–4 Settle + do-now "Say your patent's grant year and expiration year out loud to a neighbor without looking." Presentation content check.
    4–35 Lab (finish, Go Further) Finished models move to appearance and render, or the Drawing challenge. Unfinished models get the remaining time; triage again if needed.
    35–48 Presentation prep (steps 13–14) Outline against the four-part table in the notebook; practice the timeline reveal once with a neighbor timing it.
    48–51 Wrap Save. Draw and post the presentation order for tomorrow so nobody is surprised at the bell.

    Day 5 — Presentations

    Minutes Segment What happens
    0–3 Settle First presenter loads Fusion while the class settles. Class rule on the board: one question per presenter, asked by someone who hasn't asked yet.
    3–48 Presentations Three minutes plus one question, about four minutes per student. Run a visible timer. Score on the rubric as they go; the timeline reveal is the row to watch.
    48–51 Wrap Cold-close: "Which patent on the menu would you have picked now that you've seen them all built?" Preview the next unit or the overflow day.

    Presentations for a class larger than about eleven spill into a sixth day; plan the overflow as a work day for students who need it and presentations for the rest.

    ELEOT: The 20-Minute Window

    Item The move in this lesson
    B.2 No recipe: students have the tools from the tutorials and a claim to satisfy, with a pacing table that says where "attainable" is each day.
    B.3 The rubric's 4 row names a timeline that plays back as a story; the Day 3 playback is students checking their own work against it.
    B.4 Reading claim 1 and deciding which elements are essential is analysis, and the Day 3 triage conversation is evaluation under a deadline.
    B.5 Students choose the patent, write the plan, and manage three work days against the pacing table; the teacher's role is check-ins, not steps.
    C.2 The plan is required to be written before modeling and is expected to be wrong in places ("warts and all" in Turn It In); being wrong on paper is the point.
    D.2 Every artifact is a real filing by a real inventor with real dates; the maintenance-fee discussion is about actual business decisions.
    E.1 The element checklist in the notebook and the day-by-day pacing table are self-monitoring tools; the Day 3 do-now counts unchecked elements.
    E.2 Notebook check-ins on Days 2 and 3 compare plan to screen; the Day 4 practice run gets a neighbor's timing feedback before Day 5.
    E.4 Rubric in the assignment; reference it on Day 1 when defining "implements" and on Day 3 when the timeline gets renamed.
    F.3 Work days open with a one-line do-now that points at the day's target; transitions are the pacing table, posted.
    G.2 Fusion is used to create an original CAD artifact from a specification; Google Patents is used for research on Day 1.

    Common Student Errors

    These are predicted from the design and the lab's warning callouts, not observed in class; revise after the first teach.

    • Opening Fusion before reading the claim. Presents as a model that looks like the invention but is missing a claimed element (the hinge, the thumb wheel). Ask "which claim 1 element is this?" rather than pointing at the gap.
    • Eyeballed, unconstrained sketches. Blue sketch geometry that shifts when a later feature is edited. Send them back to D on the sketch; the Day 3 playback usually exposes it.
    • Uncalibrated canvas. The drawing is placed but the model is built at the image's pixel scale, so the banana case is 4 mm long. Right-click the canvas in the Browser and Calibrate against a known dimension.
    • A twelve-element off-menu patent. Students who searched on their own and picked something with a long claim 1. Catch it at approval on Day 1; after Day 2, triage to the essential elements and note the scope change in the notebook.
    • Renaming skipped until Day 4. A timeline of "Extrude12" the day before presentations. Make renaming part of the Day 3 do-now, and point at the rubric's 4 row.
    • The timeline reveal that errors live. A feature failing during playback on Day 5. The Day 4 practice run exists to catch this; hold the line on it.

    Assessment & Evidence

    • Deliverable: Fusion design Patent <number> - <short name> in the Fusion Labs project; notebook entry with patent number and link, inventor, filing, grant, and expiration dates with how it expired, claim 1 element list, pre-modeling plan, and a screenshot; the three-minute presentation (see Turn It In).
    • Score: 0–4 holistic rubric in the assignment. A 4 is every claim 1 element in the drawings' proportions, dimensioned sketches, a named timeline that plays back as a story, a plan written before modeling, and a presentation that covers all four parts including how the patent expired.
    • Look-fors during check-ins: an element list with items being checked off; a canvas that's calibrated; black (constrained) sketch geometry; Browser names that match the element list; a student who can say which element comes next and why without looking at the screen.
